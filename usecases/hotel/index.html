Wallet Passes built for Boutique Hotel

Business
Boutique hotel
Country
United Arab Emirates
Time
3 months
Revenue growth
41,000 AED

Challenge

After checkout, guests vanished into email newsletters they never opened. The spa and in-house restaurant had spare capacity during the stay, and return bookings were left to booking.com. Front desk still printed paper welcome cards that were thrown away in the room.

Strategy

A Wallet Pass was issued at confirmation, not at checkout. Stay dates, room type, and a spa credit sat on the pass. During the stay, guests received one relevant push a day at most: breakfast hours, a spa slot, or a late checkout offer. After departure, a return-stay bonus appeared on the same pass instead of a new email sequence.

Implementation

Pass before arrival

The confirmation email and WhatsApp message included a link to install the Wallet Pass. Check-in instructions and the reservation QR lived on the pass, so guests did not hunt through a mailbox at the lobby desk.

In-stay offers

Spa and F&B offers were written to the back of the pass and announced with a single push, timed to the guest’s stay, not to a generic campaign calendar.

Return stays

Seven days after checkout, the pass showed a bonus valid for a direct booking in the next 90 days. Geo was used only for guests still in the city, with a restaurant invitation the same evening.

Results

In a 3-month high season window:

  • Spa uptake. In-stay spa bookings rose by 36%.
  • F&B spend. Average food and beverage spend per stay increased by 19%.
  • Direct returns. Return bookings made on the hotel’s own link grew by 14%.

Conclusion

The hotel kept its property-management system and added a guest channel that does not compete with the lock screen. The pass is useful during the stay and still there when it is time to come back.
